

**The original transformer used ReLU in its feed-forward layers. That was 2017. In 2020, Noam Shazeer — one of the "Attention is All You Need" authors — quietly published a 4-page paper suggesting we could do better. He was right.**

The paper (Shazeer, 2020) explores a family of gated activation functions called GLU variants for the transformer's feed-forward sublayers. The core idea: instead of a simple `Linear → ReLU → Linear` block, use a gating mechanism where one linear projection controls how much of another linear projection passes through — element-wise multiplication with a learned gate. Swap different activation functions into the gate, and you get ReGLU, GEGLU, SwiGLU.

SwiGLU — using the Swish activation in the gate — came out consistently best across the experiments. The gains aren't dramatic on any single benchmark, but they're consistent across tasks and model sizes, which is exactly the kind of signal that justifies a permanent architectural change.

What makes this paper unusual is its honesty. Shazeer explicitly writes that he has "no theoretical explanation" for why SwiGLU works better than the alternatives. It just does, empirically, and he's straightforward about that. No overclaiming.

The cost: SwiGLU requires three weight matrices instead of two in the FFN block, so to keep parameter count constant you need to reduce the hidden dimension slightly. A real tradeoff — but one that virtually every major model decided was worth it.

**Why it matters now:** SwiGLU is the activation function in PaLM, LLaMA, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen, and DeepSeek. Paired with RoPE and RMSNorm — both of which we've covered in this series — it forms the architectural trinity that replaced almost every design choice from the original 2017 transformer. Three papers, three components, one modern architecture. This is the last piece of that puzzle.

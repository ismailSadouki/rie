
**The angle: "Bigger isn't always better — alignment matters more than scale"**

The paper's most striking result is that a 1.3B InstructGPT model was _preferred by humans_ over a 175B GPT-3 model. That's a powerful, counterintuitive finding that lands with any audience — engineers, recruiters, and general tech readers all get why that's surprising.

Here's a draft:

---

**GPT-3 has 175 billion parameters. InstructGPT has 1.3 billion. Humans preferred InstructGPT.**

That's the result that stopped me while reading OpenAI's InstructGPT paper.

The idea is simple: a bigger model that does what _it was trained to predict_ is less useful than a smaller model trained to do what _humans actually want_. The gap between "predict the next token" and "follow instructions helpfully" turns out to be enormous.

They closed that gap with RLHF — human labelers ranked model outputs, those rankings trained a reward model, and that reward model guided further fine-tuning via RL. Three steps. That's the recipe behind ChatGPT.

What I find underappreciated: the paper also shows RLHF models are _more truthful_ and produce _less toxic_ output than the base model — not just more helpful. Alignment and capability aren't always in tension.

The open question it leaves me with: how much of this scales? DPO and newer methods are now replacing the RL step entirely — suggesting the reward model itself might be the bottleneck.

If you work in AI and haven't read this paper, it's worth your afternoon.

_Ouyang et al., 2022 — "Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback" — arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155_

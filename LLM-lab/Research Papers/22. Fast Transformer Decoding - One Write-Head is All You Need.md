

**Multi-head attention has one expensive problem at inference time: for every token you generate, you have to reload the entire KV cache for every single attention head. MQA fixes that with a brutally simple idea.**

The paper (Shazeer, 2019) proposes Multi-Query Attention, where keys and values are shared across all attention heads instead of each head maintaining its own separate K and V matrices. Queries stay separate per head — so the expressive power of attention is mostly preserved — but the memory bandwidth cost of loading K and V at each decoding step drops dramatically.

To understand why this matters, you need to understand where the bottleneck actually is. During training, the whole sequence is processed in parallel — attention is fast. But during inference, you generate one token at a time, and at each step you must load the accumulated K and V tensors for the entire sequence so far. At long context lengths and large batch sizes, this memory bandwidth cost — not compute — becomes the dominant constraint on speed. MQA attacks that bottleneck directly.

Experimental results confirm the resulting models are significantly faster to decode, with only minor quality degradation compared to the standard multi-head baseline. The tradeoff is real but small enough that almost everyone decided it was worth it.

The honest limitation: sharing K and V across all heads does lose some representational capacity. Later work like GQA (Grouped Query Attention, Ainslie et al., 2023) found a middle ground — group heads into clusters that share K and V — which became the default in LLaMA 2, Mistral, and most modern models.

**Why it matters now:** MQA and its successor GQA are deployed in virtually every production LLM serving system in 2026. When you're streaming tokens from Claude or GPT-4 and it feels fast, KV cache optimization — starting with this paper — is a big part of why. It's also directly relevant if you're thinking about efficient inference for low-resource language models, where latency and memory constraints are even tighter.

_Shazeer, 2019 — "Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You Need" — arxiv.org/abs/1911.02150_


# What problem is it solving?

Before this paper:

- Word representations were **sparse** (one-hot vectors)
- Or based on heavy probabilistic models (slow, expensive)
- Poor at capturing semantic similarity

Goal:

> Learn dense vector representations of words where _meaning is encoded geometrically_.

Example:

- “king” close to “queen”
- “Paris” close to “France”
# 2. Key idea

Instead of modeling full language probability, they simplify:

> Learn word embeddings by predicting context words from a target word (or vice versa)

This is called the **distributional hypothesis**:

> “Words that appear in similar contexts have similar meanings.”

# 3. Two main models

## (A) CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words)

Predict center word from context:

Example:

> “the cat sits on the ___”

Input: [the, cat, sits, on, the]  
Output: “mat”

It averages surrounding word vectors.
## (B) Skip-gram (more important)

Predict context words from a center word:

Example:

> Input: “cat”  
> Output: “the, sits, on, mat”

This is the model that became most famous.


# 4. Why is it “efficient”?

The paper introduces two key tricks:

## 1. Hierarchical Softmax

Instead of computing softmax over entire vocabulary (very expensive), they use a binary tree structure.

Cost:

- Standard softmax: O(V)
- Hierarchical softmax: O(log V)
## 2. Negative Sampling (most important idea)

Instead of predicting all words, you:

- Train model to distinguish:
    - real context pairs (positive samples)
    - random word pairs (negative samples)

Objective:

- increase similarity for real pairs
- decrease similarity for fake pairs

This made training scalable to billions of words.

# 5. What is learned?

The model learns a vector space where:

## Semantic structure emerges naturally:

- king - man + woman ≈ queen
- Paris - France + Italy ≈ Rome

This is NOT explicitly programmed — it emerges from data.



# On Dareja
Skip-gram is often better than CBOW for Algerian Darja because it handles rare and slang-heavy words better, but the real improvement today comes from subword and transformer-based models rather than either method.

# 6. Why I Would Not Tell You to Go 100% Professor-Hunting Either

Because pure academic search has its own risks.

**1. Professors often have no funding** — A professor may like your profile but say "I can supervise, but I have no budget." If you cannot afford relocation, that opportunity is useless.

**2. Cold emails have low response rates** — Even good emails may get ignored. You need volume and patience.

**3. Some professors only take students from their own university** — Especially in Switzerland, Netherlands, Germany, and UK.

**4. Supervision may be weak** — Some professors accept interns but then disappear. Your real supervisor may be a PhD student. That can be okay, but only if the PhD/postdoc is active and responsive.

**5. Academic topics can become too theoretical or vague** — You need a concrete PFE deliverable. A vague topic like "Study large language models" is not enough. A good topic is: "Evaluate retrieval and hallucination failure modes in French/Arabic RAG systems using multiple retrievers, rerankers, and LLMs."

# 15. The "Professor Willing to Supervise Me" Mindset Is Slightly Wrong

Do not think: "I need to find a professor willing to supervise me."

Think: "I need to find a research team with an active problem where I can contribute enough in 5–6 months to justify supervision."

Professors do not usually want extra supervision burden. They want someone who helps with experiments, code, benchmarks, data, evaluation, reproduction, paper support.

So your pitch should be value-based.

**Bad positioning:** "Please supervise me, I am passionate about AI."

**Better positioning:** "I can help your group run experiments on multilingual LLM/RAG evaluation. I already built a small evaluation pipeline and can adapt it to your project."

That is much stronger.

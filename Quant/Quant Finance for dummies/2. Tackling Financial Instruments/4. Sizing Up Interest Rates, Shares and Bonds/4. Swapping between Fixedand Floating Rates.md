Interest-rate swaps are one of the most important financial products and one of
the first financial derivatives to gain widespread acceptance. An interest-rate swap is an agreement between two parties such as banks or large companies to trade a stream of income at a fixed rate of interest for a stream of income at a floating rate of interest. They’re traded over the counter (OTC) and not on exchanges. In an OTC trade, banks enter agreements directly with other banks or with a specialist swap dealer.


The floating rate is most frequently the six-month LIBOR

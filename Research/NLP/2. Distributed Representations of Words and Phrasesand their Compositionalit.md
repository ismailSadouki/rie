

> [!Note] Abstract
> The recently introduced continuous Skip-gram model is an efficient method for
learning high-quality distributed vector representations that capture a large num-ber of precise syntactic and semantic word relationships. In this paper we present several extensions that improve both the quality of the vectors and the training speed. By subsampling of the frequent words we obtain significant speedup and also learn more regular word representations. We also describe a simple alternative to the hierarchical softmax called negative sampling. 
An inherent limitation of word representations is their indifference to word order and their inability to represent idiomatic phrases. For example, the meanings of “Canada” and “Air” cannot be easily combined to obtain “Air Canada”. Motivated by this example, we present a simple method for finding phrases in text, and show that learning good vector representations for millions of phrases is possible.




# 1. What problem is it solving?

Earlier word2vec:

- learns vectors for **single words only**
- struggles with multi-word expressions like:
    - “New York”
    - “machine learning”
    - “United States”

Problem:

> treating phrases as separate words loses meaning

# 2. Key idea 1 — Phrase learning

They automatically detect phrases like:

- “New_York”
- “ice_cream”
- “artificial_intelligence”

Instead of treating them as separate words:

> they merge frequent word pairs into single tokens

## How phrases are selected (simple intuition)

They use a frequency-based scoring idea:

> if two words appear together often more than expected → treat as phrase

So:

- “New + York” → becomes “New_York”
- “machine + learning” → becomes “machine_learning”
# 3. Key idea 2 — Compositionality (very important)

They show that word vectors have **linear structure**.

## Famous example:

vector(king)−vector(man)+vector(woman)≈vector(queen)\text{vector}(king) - \text{vector}(man) + \text{vector}(woman) \approx \text{vector}(queen)vector(king)−vector(man)+vector(woman)≈vector(queen)

# 4. Why this works (intuition)

Word vectors capture **relationships**, not just meaning:

- gender direction: man → woman
- country → capital relation
- verb tense patterns

So:

> relationships become directions in vector space

# 5. What is “compositionality”?

It means:

> meaning of phrases or expressions can be built by combining word vectors

But word2vec shows:

- not just addition of meanings
- but structured semantic transformations
# 6. How training is done

They still use word2vec models:

- Skip-gram (mainly)
- Negative sampling

But with:

- improved preprocessing (phrase detection)



**In January 2025, a Chinese lab released a reasoning model that matched OpenAI o1 — trained with pure RL, no human-annotated reasoning traces, and open-sourced weights. The field had to pay attention.**

That's DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025), and it's one of the most consequential papers of the last two years.

The story starts with R1-Zero — an experiment where they applied reinforcement learning directly to a base model with zero supervised fine-tuning. No human-written chain-of-thought examples. No labeled reasoning trajectories. Just a rule-based reward signal: is the final answer correct? Is the format right? Nothing else.

What emerged was striking. The model spontaneously developed self-verification, reflection, and dynamic strategy exploration — including an "aha moment" where it learned to pause and reconsider its own reasoning mid-generation, producing outputs like "Wait, let me reconsider..." Nobody taught it that. The behavior emerged from the reward signal alone.

R1-Zero had problems though — poor readability and language mixing. So they built DeepSeek-R1 on top: a multi-stage pipeline with cold-start data and two rounds of RL, producing a model that matches o1 on math, coding, and STEM reasoning benchmarks.

The honest limitation: rule-based rewards only work when correctness is verifiable. Math has a right answer. Code either runs or it doesn't. For open-ended tasks — writing, judgment, nuanced reasoning — this training signal breaks down entirely. RL for reasoning is still largely limited to domains where you can check the answer automatically.

**Why it matters now:** DeepSeek-R1 did three things simultaneously: proved that reasoning can emerge from pure RL without human demonstrations, matched a frontier closed model, and released the weights openly. It democratized reasoning model research overnight and triggered a wave of replication attempts across every major lab. If you're studying LLMs in 2026, this paper is not optional.

_DeepSeek-AI, 2025 — "DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning" — arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948_

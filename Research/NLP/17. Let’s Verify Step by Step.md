

**A model can get the right answer for the wrong reason. Outcome supervision can't catch that. Process supervision can.**

That's the core problem this OpenAI paper (Lightman et al., 2023) is solving — and it's a more fundamental issue than it sounds.

The standard way to train a reward model is outcome supervision: label the final answer as correct or incorrect, and let the model figure out how to get there. Simple, scalable, cheap. The problem is that models regularly produce incorrect reasoning chains that happen to arrive at the correct answer — and outcome supervision rewards them for it. You're training the model to win, not to think correctly.

Process supervision flips this: instead of labeling only the final answer, human annotators label every individual reasoning step as correct or incorrect. The reward model — a PRM, process reward model — then scores each step independently. The result is a verifier that can catch errors mid-reasoning, before they propagate into a wrong answer.

The numbers are stark. On the challenging MATH benchmark, a PRM trained with process supervision significantly outperforms an equivalent outcome reward model, especially on harder problems where multi-step reasoning errors compound the most. They also released PRM800K — 800,000 step-level human annotations — which became a foundational dataset for subsequent reasoning research.

The honest limitation: step-level human annotation is expensive and doesn't scale easily beyond math and formal reasoning, where correctness at each step is unambiguous. For open-ended tasks, defining what a "correct intermediate step" even means is genuinely hard.

**Why it matters now:** This paper is the theoretical backbone of how modern reasoning models are trained and evaluated. DeepSeek-R1's rule-based rewards, o1's verifier, and the entire test-time compute scaling literature all depend on the PRM framing this paper established. If my last few posts traced a thread from CoT → Self-Consistency → DeepSeek-R1, this paper is what connects them all underneath.

# One thing to pay attention to

While reading, compare everything with DeepSeek-R1.

DeepSeek-R1 mainly uses:

```
Question
      ↓
Reasoning
      ↓
Final answer
      ↓
Reward
```

This paper asks:

> Why wait until the end?

Instead:

```
Step 1
↓

Reward

↓

Step 2

↓

Reward

↓

Step 3

↓

Reward
```

That single change is the core contribution of the paper.

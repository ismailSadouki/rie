**Instruction finetuning cost Google 0.2% of pre-training compute. The performance gains were massive.**

That's the quiet bombshell in Google's Flan paper (Chung et al., 2022) — and it's arguably the recipe that made modern LLMs actually usable.

The setup: take a pretrained model (PaLM 540B), finetune it on 1,836 tasks phrased as instructions, evaluate on held-out benchmarks. Result: +9.4% average improvement — for essentially a rounding error in compute cost.

The finding I keep thinking about is the reasoning degradation problem. Finetune on instructions _without_ chain-of-thought (CoT) examples, and reasoning performance actually _drops_. The model gets better at following instructions but worse at thinking. Fix? Add just 9 CoT datasets to the mix. That's it.

The other underrated result: a 3B Flan-T5 beats GPT-3 175B on MMLU. Fifty times smaller.

**Why it matters in 2026:** Every instruction-tuned model you use today — GPT-4, Gemini, Claude — traces its DNA back to this line of work. The Flan recipe (diverse tasks + CoT data + scale) became the default starting point before RLHF and DPO entered the picture. Understanding it is understanding the foundation everything else is built on.

The open question that's still unresolved: as base models get stronger, does instruction finetuning become a smaller multiplier — or a bigger one?

**Instruction fine-tuning with CoT teaches the model to generate reasoning on its own, even when no reasoning examples are provided at inference time (zero-shot).**

Before instruction tuning, models often needed carefully crafted few-shot prompts to reason well.

After instruction tuning on CoT data, the model has **learned the reasoning pattern itself**, so a simple prompt like **"Let's think step by step"** is often enough.

**Instruction fine-tuning with Chain-of-Thought enables zero-shot reasoning: the model can generate its own reasoning steps for unseen tasks without requiring few-shot CoT examples.**

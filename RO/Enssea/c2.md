# 2.6 Stationary Markov Chain
**Definition:**

A chain is **stationary** (or regular) if and only if it is **irreducible** (single equivalence class) and aperiodic (d=1).

**Theorem:**

If the chain is stationary, there exists a unique stationary distribution π solving:
![](https://i.imgur.com/RBHdsmW.png)
The stationary chain is also ergodic.

**Ergodicity Conditions**
A Markov chain is ergodic if it converges to a unique stationary distribution π\piπ.

**Theorem:** Every regular matrix (chain) is ergodic.

A regular Markov chain converges quickly and uniformly to the stationary distribution, while an ergodic chain may converge more slowly or non-uniformly.


Here is a **clear, exam-level explanation**, with the **intuition and distinctions** made explicit.

---

## Theorem

**Every regular Markov chain (or regular stochastic matrix) is ergodic.**

---

## Definitions (to fix ideas)

- A **regular** Markov chain means:  
    [  
    \exists n \ge 1 \quad \text{such that} \quad P^n > 0  
    ]  
    (all entries of ( P^n ) are strictly positive).
    
- An **ergodic** Markov chain means:
    
- the chain is **irreducible**, and
    
- it admits a **unique stationary distribution** ( \pi ), and
    
- time averages converge to space averages (long-run frequencies equal ( \pi )).
    

(Equivalently, in many notes: irreducible + positive recurrent.)

---

## Why Every Regular Chain Is Ergodic

### Step 1: Regular ⇒ Irreducible

If ( P^n > 0 ), then:  
[  
(P^n)_{ij} > 0 \quad \forall i,j  
]

So:

- every state can reach every other state in exactly ( n ) steps
    
- the chain has **one communication class**
    

✔ Hence, the chain is **irreducible**.

---

### Step 2: Regular ⇒ Aperiodic

Since:  
[  
(P^n)_{ii} > 0 \quad \forall i  
]

- state ( i ) can return to itself in ( n ) steps
    
- the gcd of return times is 1
    

✔ Hence, the chain is **aperiodic**.

---

### Step 3: Irreducible + Aperiodic ⇒ Unique Stationary Distribution

By classical Markov chain theory (or Perron–Frobenius):

- there exists a **unique stationary distribution** ( \pi )
    
- ( \pi_i > 0 ) for all states
    
- for any initial distribution ( \mu ):  
    [  
    \mu P^k \to \pi  
    ]
    

✔ This is exactly **ergodicity**.

---

## Conclusion of the Theorem

[  
\boxed{\text{Regular} ;\Rightarrow; \text{Ergodic}}  
]

Every regular chain satisfies all conditions needed for ergodicity.

---

## Why the Converse Is Not Always True

An **ergodic** chain does **not necessarily** satisfy:  
[  
\exists n \text{ such that } P^n > 0  
]

So:  
[  
\text{ergodic} ;\nRightarrow; \text{regular}  
]

This explains the second statement.

---

## Convergence Behavior: Regular vs Ergodic

### Regular Markov Chain

- Strong mixing
    
- Uniform convergence:  
    [  
    \sup_i \left| P^k(i,\cdot) - \pi \right| \to 0  
    ]
    
- Fast (often exponential) convergence
    
- No dependence on initial state
    

📌 **Very strong notion of stability**

---

### Ergodic Markov Chain

- Converges to ( \pi )
    
- Convergence may be:
    
    - slower
        
    - non-uniform across states
        
- Some transition probabilities may remain zero for all finite powers
    

📌 **Weaker mixing condition**

---

## Intuition Summary

- **Regular** ⇒ “after some time, everything talks to everything”
    
- **Ergodic** ⇒ “long-run averages stabilize”
    
- Regularity guarantees **stronger and faster convergence**
    
- Ergodicity guarantees convergence, but not how fast or how uniformly
    

---

## Key Takeaway (Exam-Ready)

> Every regular Markov chain is ergodic because regularity implies irreducibility and aperiodicity, which guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a stationary distribution and convergence toward it. However, ergodic chains may converge more slowly or non-uniformly than regular ones.

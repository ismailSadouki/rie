When you want to compare **more than two machine learning models** using **resampling methods** like cross-validation, you can't just compare their average performance independently — because **their scores are correlated**. ### 🔍 The Problem:

Let’s say you evaluate 3 models (A, B, and C) using **10-fold cross-validation**:

- All models are tested on the **same 10 folds**.
    
- So, the performance scores (like AUC, accuracy) across models are **not independent**.
    
- This **within-resample correlation** must be accounted for. Otherwise, statistical tests may:
    
    - Overestimate differences
        
    - Give misleading significance levels
        

---

### ✅ The Solution:

Use **advanced statistical modeling techniques** that take this dependency into account.

#### 1. 🧠 **Bayesian Hierarchical Models**

(**McElreath, 2015** — from _"Statistical Rethinking"_)

- Treats the model performances as coming from a shared probability distribution.
    
- Models both:
    
    - Variation **within resamples**
        
    - And differences **between models**
        
- Useful when:
    
    - Comparing many models
        
    - Interested in **probabilistic interpretation** (e.g., "Model A is better than B with 90% probability")
        

#### 2. ♻️ **Repeated Measures Models**

(**West & Galecki, 2014**)

- Similar to ANOVA but adapted for correlated, repeated measures.
    
- Each fold (or resample) is treated like a repeated "measurement" across models.
    
- Allows testing if model differences are statistically significant, **considering fold-to-fold variation**.
    

#### 3. 📊 **Original Idea – Hothorn et al. (2005)**

- Proposed rigorous frameworks for model comparison in resampling.
    
- Often implemented in R packages like `mlr` or `caret`.
    

#### 4. 🧮 **Benavoli et al. (2016)**

- Proposed **Bayesian signed-rank tests** to compare models on **multiple datasets** or resamples.
    
- Advantage:
    
    - Provides **posterior probabilities** of one model outperforming another (e.g., “Model X is better than Y 85% of the time”)
        
    - Avoids traditional p-values
        
    - Good for **pairwise or multiple model comparisons**
